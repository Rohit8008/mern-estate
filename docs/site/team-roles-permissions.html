Team, roles and permissions

Adding people, choosing what each can do, how access is scoped to categories and records, and where to look when something goes wrong.

Two layers of access

What a person can do is decided by two separate things. Mixing them up is the most common source of "why can't they see it".

  1. Account type (user.role): admin, employee, seller, buyer (the schema also allows user, the default). Only admin and employee can enter the CRM (the CRM shell requires one of the two).
  2. Custom role (user.assignedRole): a named bundle of permissions that you assign to an employee. Admins bypass permission checks entirely.

Then a third thing narrows the data: scoping (which categories, listings and leads an employee may touch).

Account typeWhat it means
adminAdmin of one workspace, not the vendor. Sees everything in that workspace, passes every permission check, manages users, roles and settings. Not the same as a platform operator (see Multi-tenancy).
employeeAgency staff. Gets only the permissions of the custom role assigned to them, and sees only data in their scope.
sellerSees only listings they created. No CRM.
buyerNo CRM; may not create, edit or delete listings.

An employee with no custom role can do almost nothing. The permission check answers 403 "No role assigned. Access denied." and the app's permission context resolves to an empty set, so the CRM screens redirect to "Unauthorized". The single exception is creating listings, which an employee with no role is allowed (category restrictions still apply). Always assign a role right after inviting an employee.

Add a team member

To invite an employee: Admin Panel (/admin), Users tab, Create Employee: username, email, optional phone, and the categories they are assigned to. Admin only. Then assign a role in the Roles & Permissions tab.

  • Counts against the plan's seat limit maxUsers (trial 3, starter 10, growth 40, enterprise unlimited). At the cap the request answers 402 before anything is created.
  • Email must be unused in the workspace (409 otherwise); a phone number must also be unused.
  • No password is ever chosen or emailed. The account is created with a random password nobody sees, and an invitation is sent.

Invitations

  • The invite email links to /invite/:token. The token is 32 random bytes, stored only as a SHA-256 hash, valid for 7 days and single-use. The link carries the workspace, so the recipient lands in the right agency without knowing the word "workspace".
  • The invitee chooses a password and must accept the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy. The acceptance records the legal version and time. They are then signed in directly, and the email tells them the workspace address to use on later sign-ins.
  • An unknown, expired or already-used token all show the same "no longer valid" message, so tokens cannot be probed.
  • Only the newest invitation works. Sending a fresh invite remembers up to four earlier links; opening one of those answers 410 with code INVITE_REPLACED ("A newer invitation was sent to you") so the invitee knows to open the newer email.
  • If mail is not configured the invite cannot be delivered. The create call returns the link (inviteUrl, with inviteSent) and the Admin Panel shows it with a Copy button so an admin can pass it on. Employees who have not accepted show a Resend invite button (POST /api/user/employee/:id/invite, admin only), which mints a new link and kills the old one. An admin can also set an employee's password directly (below).

Manage existing users

In the Users tab, open Manage on a person. All of these are admin-only and refuse to act on yourself or on another admin:

ActionRules
Change account type and assigned categoriesPOST /api/user/role/:id. You cannot change your own type or another admin's.
Deactivate or reactivateStatus active / inactive. An inactive account cannot sign in ("Your account is inactive"). Not allowed on admins or yourself.
Set an employee's passwordEmployees only (not admins, not yourself). The password must meet the rules below. It clears pending reset codes and signs the employee out everywhere (refresh tokens are cleared). A security-log entry admin_password_reset is written.
DeleteSoft delete, not on admins or yourself.

A person can close their own account from Settings, Privacy (Close Account) or their Profile; that erases their personal details and keeps the records they created, attributed to "Former team member". See Data rights.

Password rules and lockout

  • At least 8 characters with an uppercase letter, a lowercase letter, a digit and a special character from ! @ # $ % ^ & * ( ) , . ? " : { } | < >. The same check applies to invites, password changes, resets and admin resets.
  • After 5 failed sign-ins (MAX_LOGIN_ATTEMPTS) the account is locked for 15 minutes (LOCKOUT_DURATION); a successful sign-in resets the counter. Auth endpoints are rate-limited (20 requests per 15 minutes by default in production).
  • Sessions: 15-minute access token and 30-day refresh token in httpOnly cookies. See Authentication.

Custom roles

Roles live under Admin Panel, Roles & Permissions (RoleManagement). The whole /api/roles API is admin-only, whatever the manageRoles permission says.

  • Create a role: a unique name (per workspace), a description (up to 500 characters) and ticked permissions. All permissions default to off.
  • Assign / remove: choose a role for a user (POST /api/roles/assign, /remove). You cannot assign an inactive role.
  • Members: open a role to list its members (GET /api/roles/:id/users).
  • Edit: a role marked isSystem cannot be renamed or deactivated; its permissions can still be edited.
  • Delete: system roles cannot be deleted. A role with members answers 400 "Cannot delete role. N user(s) are assigned…"; confirming a force delete (?force=true) unassigns everyone and then soft-deletes the role. Those employees are left with no role, which locks them out of most of the CRM (see the warning above).
  • Restore default roles: the button calls POST /api/roles/initialize-defaults, which creates any of the five built-in roles that do not already exist by name and leaves existing ones untouched. The old GET on the same path still works as a deprecated alias (it answers with a Deprecation header).
Built-in rolePermissions it grants
Super Admin (system)All 35.
Employee (system)viewClients, viewOwners, createListing, viewListings, viewCategories, viewMessages, sendMessages, viewBuyerRequirements, uploadFiles
Listing Manager (system)createOwner, updateOwner, viewOwners, toggleOwnerActive, createListing, updateListing, viewListings, publishListing, viewCategories, viewMessages, sendMessages, viewBuyerRequirements, uploadFiles

The built-in roles are defined once in backend/utils/defaultRoles.js (Super Admin, Sales Manager, Listing Manager, Employee, Viewer); both the restore button and npm run db:seed-roles read it, so they create the same five. The table above lists three of them.

Role documents are cached per process for 60 seconds (requirePermission), so a permission change can take up to a minute to reach every API call. The app fetches an employee's permissions once per session load (/api/user/my-permissions), so a signed-in employee sees menu changes after a reload.

How requirePermission works

router.get('/', requirePermission('viewClients'), getClients);
  1. If the account type is admin, it passes.
  2. Otherwise the user needs an assignedRole, else 403 "No role assigned".
  3. The role is loaded (cached 60 s). It must exist and be active, else 403 "Invalid or inactive role".
  4. The role must have the key set to true, else 403 "Permission denied. Required permission: <key>".

The frontend mirrors this for navigation only: the sidebar is the intersection of the screen catalogue, the workspace's switched-on screens and the user's permissions (useScreens), and PermissionRoute redirects to "Unauthorized". The UI is not a security boundary; the API enforces. Screens with a requires key: Dashboard and Analytics and Transactions (viewAnalytics), All Properties (viewListings), Categories (viewCategories), Add Property (createListing), Clients and Sales Pipeline (viewClients), Property Owners (viewOwners), Buyer Requirements (viewBuyerRequirements), Client Reports (exportData). Tasks and Calendar require none. Import from Excel, Admin Panel and Settings are admin-only.

Employee scoping

Permissions say what kind of thing you may do; scoping says which records.

Listings

listingScope(user) returns a database filter that every multi-listing read path (list, search, suggestions, facets, export, buyer matching, share creation) merges in. The UI is not relied on.

Account typeListings visible
adminAll in the workspace.
employeeListings whose assignedAgent is them, which they created (userRef), or which sit in one of their assigned categories.
sellerOnly what they created.
buyerNothing: the function returns a filter that matches no listing for buyers and anonymous callers (the property book is not public). Only admins and employees can create share links.

Single-record access (canAccessListing) follows the same rules; for updates and voice notes the assigned agent also counts. An employee can create a listing only in an assigned category. Assigned categories are set when the employee is created and changed with Manage. Use POST /api/listing/assign-agent (admin-only) to give a specific agent a specific listing.

Leads, tasks and the rest

  • Leads: an employee sees and edits only leads assigned to them; reassigning is admin-only.
  • Tasks: own tasks; admin can see and assign across the team.
  • Documents: admin: all; others only on parents they own or work on (their leads, their listings, their assigned categories).
  • Buyer requirements: admin and employee see every requirement in the workspace; sellers and buyers see only their own.
  • Share links: an admin sees all links, anyone else only their own.

Two endpoints are looser than the lead scope: a data-rights contact export is authorised by exportData alone, and viewClients opens the tasks and transactions APIs. Grant those permissions deliberately.

Checklist: onboarding an agent

  1. Check seats: Settings, Workspace shows plan usage; maxUsers blocks the invite at the cap.
  2. Admin Panel, Users, Create Employee (choose their categories).
  3. Roles & Permissions: assign a role (restore the defaults first if the list is empty).
  4. Ask them to open the invite within 7 days and choose a password.
  5. If the sidebar is missing items, check the role's permissions and whether the workspace switched the screen off (Screens).

API: see the Roles and Tenant references.
